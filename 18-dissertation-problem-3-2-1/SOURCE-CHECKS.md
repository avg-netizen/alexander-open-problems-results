## Alexander's later ill-founded examples

Samuel Allen Alexander, *Self-referential theories*, Journal of Symbolic Logic **85**(4) (2020), 1687–1716. Inspected [arXiv:2008.11535v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11535v1), Corollary 78 and Theorem 79 with proof, author-version pp. 23–25.

The cited existence of true families over certain ill-founded recursively enumerable partial orders does not provide a computable infinite descending path satisfying this entry's hypotheses. The main paper and supplement preserve this distinction. No inconsistency with that theorem is claimed.

## Carlson and other standard inputs

Carlson's *Knowledge, machines, and the consistency of Reinhardt's strong mechanistic thesis*, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic **105** (2000), 51–82, is credited for the earlier modal framework, as identified in Alexander's source discussion and bibliography. No result in this entry is presented as a new proof of Carlson's construction.

Arithmetized syntax, fixed partial truth, ordinary logical cut elimination, witnessed derivability conditions and the second incompleteness theorem are standard inputs. The local uses and the presentation comparisons are spelled out in the papers; no unpublished lemma is required from another project directory. The source PDFs are cited and linked, not copied into this repository.
